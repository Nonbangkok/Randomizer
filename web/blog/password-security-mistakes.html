5 Password Security Mistakes You're Probably Making

Data breaches expose billions of passwords every year. The scary part? Most breached passwords are completely predictable. Here are five mistakes that put you at risk — and how to fix each one right now.

1. Reusing the Same Password

This is the #1 mistake. When one site gets breached, attackers immediately try that password on Gmail, banking sites, and social media. This is called "credential stuffing" and it's automated — they test millions of accounts in hours. Solution: Every account needs a unique password. A password manager makes this painless.

2. Using Predictable Patterns

Think "Password1!" is safe? Attackers' dictionary attacks include every common substitution: @ for a, 3 for e, ! at the end. Even "Tr0ub4dor&3" can be cracked if it's based on a word. Solution: Use truly random characters — no dictionary words as a base.

3. Weak Entropy (Too Short)

An 8-character password with uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols has about 52 bits of entropy. Modern GPUs can crack that in hours. A 16-character random password has 104 bits — cracking it would take billions of years. The math matters.

4. Using Personal Information

Birthdays, pet names, hometowns — all of this is often publicly available on social media. Sophisticated attacks against specific targets ("spearphishing") always try personal info first. Solution: Your passwords should contain zero personal information.

5. Never Rotating High-Risk Passwords

Passwords for email, banking, and primary social accounts should be rotated at least annually — or immediately after any service you use reports a breach. Check haveibeenpwned.com to see if your email appears in any known breach.
