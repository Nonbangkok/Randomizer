How to Finally Conquer Your Video Game Backlog

We've all been there: a Steam Summer Sale, a tempting Humble Bundle, or a series of Game Pass additions that leave your library overflowing with titles you've never even installed. This phenomenon, often called "gaming paralysis," occurs when the sheer volume of choices prevents you from making any choice at all.

The Psychology of Choice Overload

When faced with 100+ games, our brains struggle to determine which one will provide the "maximum" value or enjoyment. We fear picking the "wrong" game and wasting our limited free time. This leads to us spending an hour scrolling through the menu only to end up playing another round of a comfort game like Minecraft or League of Legends.

Step 1: Audit Your Library

Be honest with yourself. Not every game you own is a game you actually want to play. Some were part of bundles; others were bought on a whim years ago. Categorize your games into "Must Play," "Maybe Later," and "Never." Moving games to a "Never" category (or hidden folder) reduces the mental load immediately.

Step 2: Limit Your "Active" List

Don't try to play five RPGs at once. Limit yourself to 2-3 games of different genres (e.g., one long story-driven RPG, one casual platformer, and one competitive shooter). This ensures you always have something that fits your current mood without feeling overwhelmed.

Step 3: Let Fate Decide

Sometimes, you just need a nudge. If you have narrowed your "Must Play" list down to 5 great games and still can't decide, use a tool to break the tie. Using a Game Backlog Wheel takes the pressure of decision-making off your shoulders. Once the wheel spins, commit to playing that game for at least two hours.

Conclusion

The goal of gaming is fun, not productivity. A backlog shouldn't feel like a chore list. By auditing your library and using randomizers to break the tie, you can get back to what matters: actually playing games.
