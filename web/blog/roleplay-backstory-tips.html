5 Tips for Writing Compelling RPG Backstories

You've generated the perfect name, chosen your class, and rolled your stats. Now comes the hardest part: the backstory. A great backstory isn't just a list of events; it's the foundation of how your character reacts to the world.

1. Start with a "Why," not a "What"

Instead of listing that your character is an orphan (a classic trope), focus on why they left their home. Was it for revenge? Curiority? Or perhaps they were running away from a destiny they weren't ready for? Motivation drives roleplay better than biography.

2. Include a Weakness or Flaw

Perfect characters are boring to play and even more boring to play with. Give your character a phobia, a vice, or a social awkwardness. This gives your Game Master hooks to create engaging scenes specifically for you.

3. Connect Them to the World

Your character doesn't exist in a vacuum. Mention a specific shop, a minor noble, or a rival mercenary band. This makes the world feel lived-in and gives you immediate relevance to the campaign setting.

4. Keep it Concise

A ten-page backstory is often too much for a busy GM. Aim for "The Knife Theory": 5-7 short "knives" (hooks) that the GM can use to stab your character with drama later in the game.

5. Let the Game Fill the Gaps

You don't need to know everything about your character's past on day one. Leave some mystery even for yourself. Sometimes the best character traits emerge naturally through gameplay and random encounters.

Conclusion

A backstory is a springboard for future adventures. Use it to build a character you're excited to inhabit for months to come.
